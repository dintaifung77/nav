Briefing

Overview & Objective

This simulation replaces traditional round-robin reading with a competitive, collaborative tabletop game framework. The goal is not winning points — it is choosing.

Objective

  • Squads analyze, debate, and apply all the content and ESV verses from the original study.
  • Teams earn points for the depth of their arguments, the accuracy of their biblical analysis, and the practicality of their applications.
  • Leave with a concrete, personal next move for the week ahead.

Squads & Setup

Divide the students into small squads. Nominate a captain to keep time and a scribe to keep score. One game board per squad.

Kick off with a 5-minute warm-up: “If you were offered a crown worth more than everything you own, what would you have to leave behind to take it?”

Loadout — Materials Needed

Game board: whiteboard with dry-erase markers, or a shared digital document / large paper sheet
Printed copy or digital reference of the ESV verses for quick lookup
7 disciple marker cards
One marker per person

Snacks

Character Profile — The Table

Context / Situation

Meaning

The Hot Take Round — 3 minutes

Pose this central question to the room: “Is Jesus asking way too much here, or does this make total sense? Defend your verse.” Each team presents its verse as evidence and argues whether Jesus’ standard is overwhelmingly impossible or realistically achievable. The facilitator awards points for strong textual backing.

Level 2 · 15 min

Lore & Choice Points

15 minutes
Part 2: Mark 1 and Mark 10. Turn the two Gospel narrative texts into interactive decision points where teams must weigh the costs and choices of biblical figures.
Scenario A · Mark 1:14–20
The Early Squad

Join the Lobby

Have teams roleplay as the fishermen instantly dropping their nets.

Discussion Prompt — “What was the price tag of joining this lobby right away? If you were in their shoes, would you hit ‘accept’ or ‘decline’ and why?”
Scenario B · Mark 10:17–31
The Rich Young Ruler

The High-Stakes Dilemma

Frame this scenario as a high-stakes moral dilemma choice in a game narrative.

Discussion Prompt — “This guy had everything unlocked, but Jesus hit him with a major roadblock. Why did Jesus answer him that way? What was his actual blocker?” Teams debate whether the rich man walked away making a smart or terrible choice, and what lessons the original disciples learned from it.

The Dice Ask: Which Do You Feel More?

One squad member shares honestly: which of the two are you more like — the ones who dropped everything, or the one who kept everything? The group stays quiet and listens. No fixing, no preaching — just honesty.

Level 3 · 15 min

The IRL Inventory Builder

15 minutes
Practical application, treated as Inventory Management for Real Life. Teams equip a disciple marker and pitch one wild, creative, ultra-specific real-world scenario for living it out this week.
The Setup

The 7 Disciple Markers

Provide teams with the list of the 7 disciple markers. Each team must select one or more to equip this week. Tap a marker to highlight your pick.

The Challenge

Pitch a Real-World Scenario

Pitch one wild, creative, or ultra-specific real-world scenario of how a student can actually equip and live out that “item” this week — at school or online.

Scoring

Vote for the Best Pitch

The whole group or facilitator votes on the most practical, creative, and realistic strategy to award bonus points.

Final Boss Round

10 minutes
Walking This Out. Conclude the session with an open-mic discussion or a quick-fire response session covering the final reflection questions.
The Boss Encounter

Open Mic — Final Reflection Questions

1

Mentors & Leaders

“Who are the real-life mentors, contemporary figures, or historical leaders whose teachings you respect or follow? What do you appreciate about them?”

2

The Realistic Follower

“Do you genuinely think it’s realistic for someone to follow Jesus today? If an absolute stranger walked up to you tomorrow claiming they want to follow Jesus, what kind of vibe or traits would you realistically expect them to have based on what we just read?”

Open-Mic Format

  • Each person shares their answer to Question 1.
  • Each person shares their answer to Question 2.
  • The squad agrees on one accountability check-in before next session.

Victory Screen

Close by praying for one another — that the squad would carry the cost of the crown this week. Then present the small prizes and ring the bell: session complete.
